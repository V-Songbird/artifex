# Showcase site

The site is one film the visitor scrolls. Every section, and every seam between two sections, is a shot played by an ordinary Artifex piece; the scroll position is the playhead of the whole site. Scrolling back rewinds, because every frame is a function of the seed and the playhead.

The first thirteen shots are the site's art (see [the art shots](#the-art-shots)). The three after them (`rows`, the seam `grid`, and the still `cells`) are placeholders that keep the stage's worker tier and recipe checks exercised until the later shots replace them.

## Commands

| Command | Purpose and result |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run site` | Builds `out/site/` from `site/shots.js` and prints the brotli size of the first load and of the whole site. |
| `npm run site -- --serve [--port N]` | Builds, then serves `out/site/` on `127.0.0.1` until stopped. |
| `npm run site:check` | Runs `site/tests/*.test.js`: the builder, the refusals, the preview server, the stage's pure helpers, the kept copies' release rule, the folded reflections' pixels, the site check's pure helpers and whether every poster was drawn from its shot's current recipe. |
| `npm run posters [-- --edge PATH] [--timeout-ms N] [--headed]` | Draws every poster the shot list names in installed Edge and writes each that changed, with its recipe record beside it (see [posters](#the-art-shots)). |
| `node tools/check-site.js [--edge PATH] [--timeout-ms N] [--headed]` | Checks the built site in installed Edge; run `npm run site` first. |

The site's tests are not in `tests/*.test.js`, so the mutation suite, which reruns the whole Node suite for every mutation, does not grow with the site. The [Check workflow](../../.github/workflows/check.yml) builds the site and runs `npm run site:check`; it deploys nothing and does not run the Edge check.

## The shot list

`site/shots.js` exports `{ hz, seed, shots }`. `hz` is the site's frame rate: frames of scroll per second of film. `seed` is the seed every piece is solved with, so a seam and the shots on either side of it agree. Each shot has:

| Key | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | Kebab-case and unique; recipe URLs and script names use it. |
| `piece` | Path to a CommonJS piece, from `site/shots.js`. |
| `seam` | A transformation between two shots: hidden from assistive technology, and not shown under reduced motion. |
| `seconds` | How long a still holds. Required for a still; refused for a timed piece, which lasts its own duration. |
| `tier` | `'worker'` draws the shot off the main thread; otherwise a timed piece draws per frame and a still once per change. |
| `still` | The playhead shown under reduced motion; 1 by default. |
| `poster` | Path to a `.webp`, `.png` or `.jpg` drawn from the same recipe, from `site/shots.js`, shown until a worker-tier frame is ready and used as the reduced-motion still. The build copies it to `posters/<name>.<ext>`, and the page names only that path. |
| `title`, `text` | The copy over the shot, as HTML-escaped text. |

Unknown keys are refused. The list is resolved with `shots` from [`core/time.js`](../../core/time.js) at build time, so a shot too short to hold a whole frame fails the build.

## The art shots

Each seam begins on the last frame of the shot before it, drawn from the same state, and each shot on the last frame of its seam, so a join changes nothing on screen; every piece is solved with the site's seed. The pieces share their modules in `site/pieces/`.

| Shot | Piece | What it draws | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `intro` | `intro.cjs` | A plotter pen writes the wordmark on drawing paper, a line at a time, its gantry's shadow over the sheet; the last line stays wet. | 4.5 s |
| `bloom` (seam) | `bloom.cjs` | A wide brush of clean water crosses the wet line and the ink blooms into the water along its current. | 3.5 s |
| `ink` | `ink.cjs` | A brush lays that one ink along the water's current, pale washes first and dark strokes last, with tapered touchdowns, dry-brush breaks and gloss. | 6 s |
| `crack` (seam) | `crack.cjs` | The sheet dries a shade lighter and tears in generations of recursive cuts, each running between tears already there, with a white fibrous core and the table showing through. | 5 s |
| `mirror` | `mirror.cjs` | The torn pieces lift apart; three glass mirrors slide in and stand on the sheet round a triangle, and its reflections slide out of them, ring by ring, into the wallpaper group *333. | 7 s |
| `bend` (seam) | `bend.cjs` | The mirrors, pinned at their corners, bow inwards; the wallpaper turns hyperbolic, its reflections nesting towards one circle, and the mirrors turn together. | 6 s |
| `portal` (seam) | `portal.cjs` | A notebook page with the catalog's bird doodled in ballpoint slides in under a mirror, the kaleidoscope carries it into every reflection, and the camera goes into one mirror's reflection of it. | 7 s |
| `trace` (seam) | `trace.cjs` | The mirror lets go of its bow and the page straightens; drafting film slides over the doodle and a pen, seen by its shadow, inks the bird in one line: the branch along a T-square, the legs against a set square standing on it, the beak's sides against the set square, the eye round a circle template, the curves freehand. | 10 s |
| `print` (seam) | `print.cjs` | The page is pulled from under the film onto a sheet brushed with cyanotype; a lamp exposes it, the film is lifted away, and water poured across washes it Prussian blue with the lines white: a blueprint. | 8 s |
| `cad` | `cad.cjs` | The camera draws back to the whole blueprint while it dries; the intro's plotter, seen by its shadow, lays the rest of the CAD drawing over it a layer at a time, as a time-lapse after its first second. | 12 s |
| `fold` (seam) | `fold.cjs` | The camera goes in to the bird; a knife cuts it out part by part, the parts rise onto their own layers, the wing folds up on the plot's fold line and shows the paper's white back, and the sheet tears into strips that part and stack. | 10 s |
| `paint` (seam) | `paint.cjs` | A wide painting knife spreads a thick cream ground over the papercraft in three sweeps, the blue paper soaking up its oil and the layers raised through the ground; a brush, a stroke at a time and then as a time-lapse, lays thick impasto strokes, the swirling background patch by patch and the bird last, each stroke dragging a little of the one laid beside it. | 10 s |
| `kandinsky` | `kandinsky.cjs` | A palette knife drags the strokes off in long passes round the bird, heaping their paint along its edge and smearing it over the ground, swirls three halos, then drags, pivots and presses the bird's own paint into the Kandinsky style's forms; the long lines and floating forms come last, and the knife has left by the last frame. | 12 s |

- **One ink.** [`paper.js`](../../site/pieces/paper.js) holds the paper and one ink: `tone(c)` is the colour ink at concentration `c` leaves on the paper, and `wash(c)` the ink at the opacity that leaves it with ordinary source-over drawing. The multiply composite doubled a frame's cost on a GPU-backed canvas.
- **What stays is kept.** [`held.js`](../../site/pieces/held.js) keeps what a shot has finished drawing as copies per canvas, scale and solve: `hold` and `keep` for an opaque layer, `upTo` for marks laid one after another (a copy every fourth count and two recently used ones), `soft` for marks laid at a fraction of the resolution and smoothed back. A copy is made on the shot's first frame; when the stage lowers the scale, the copies are shrunk rather than redrawn. Copies are kept only for the three solves drawn last (on the site, the shot on screen, the one warmed beside it and the one before), and the oldest of the three only while all copies together hold at most 24 megapixels; a solve given up has its canvases emptied at once, and its copies are made again, as on its first frame, if it is drawn again. Kept for every shot, a desktop walk ended holding 40 canvases of 31.7 megapixels; now it ends with 17 of 9.6 and peaks at 22.5. Its `prime` hook draws, on the shot's own canvas under the first copy, every kind of mark the shot will make: a GPU-backed canvas costs 25 to 40 ms the first time it meets a kind of mark, and the first frame is drawn before the shot is on screen.
- **Curved mirrors, pixel by pixel.** A circle mirror reflects by inversion, which no copy of a picture can show, so [`inversion.js`](../../site/pieces/inversion.js) folds every pixel outside the region between the mirrors back into it on the CPU, at one folded pixel per 3.6 canvas pixels each way, reading the lifted scene's colours once at half the design's resolution, a little darker for every reflection and blended to the mean tone past seven. The reflections in `bend` and `portal` are therefore softer than the sheet between the mirrors; the page the camera goes into in `portal` is drawn sharp, its lines mapped through the inversion (straight lines as exact arcs). The display uses neither WebGL nor WebGPU.
- **The page** ([`page.js`](../../site/pieces/page.js)) is built from the style library's subject and helpers ([`skills/artifex/styles/subject.js`](../../skills/artifex/styles/subject.js), [`kit.js`](../../skills/artifex/styles/kit.js)) as the [doodle](../../skills/artifex/styles/doodle.md) style draws it, as polylines, so it can be drawn through any map.
- **Where the portal ends.** [`enter.js`](../../site/pieces/enter.js) draws the portal's picture for `portal.cjs` and gives the shots after it the portal's camera (`camera`) and its last view with the mirror let go (`released`): the page drawn through a blend of the inversion and a similarity fitted to the doodled bird, so the trace begins on the portal's last frame and ends on a flat page the square tracing lies on exactly.
- **One sheet, one subject, four media.** [`draft.js`](../../site/pieces/draft.js) lays out the drafting sheet in its own units (a landscape CAD sheet, the front view where the doodled bird lies) with the film, the ink's schedule and the instruments; [`blueprint.js`](../../site/pieces/blueprint.js) the cyanotype print of that film (a brushed coat, its exposure and wash); [`cadsheet.js`](../../site/pieces/cadsheet.js) the rest of the CAD drawing, following the [CAD](../../skills/artifex/styles/cad.md) style's layout, layers and dimensions, as marks in the order a plotter draws them; [`papercut.js`](../../site/pieces/papercut.js) the cut, the layers, the fold and the tears, following the [papercraft](../../skills/artifex/styles/papercraft.md) style's torn cores, clean cut edges and shadows. The CAD style's dark ground and light lines come from the print: the inked film shades a cyanotype sheet, which washes Prussian blue with white lines, on paper that can then be cut and folded, its white back and core the papercraft's cream. Every straight line of the tracing is ruled against its instrument. The sheet is kept rather than drawn each frame: the print keeps it dry, exposed and washed, at its last view, which holds every earlier one, and mixes them (the washed copy kept only behind the water, by `destination-in`); the CAD shot keeps it at its first view and its last, laying the close copy over the whole one while it draws back, and adds each mark the pen finishes to a kept copy of the sheet; the fold keeps the plotted sheet at its first view and its close one. The notebook page the print pulls away is drawn only where it is in the frame, and nothing under it while it covers the frame. A browser drops what an opaque copy then covers whole, the fold's first-frame marks included, so the fold copies a pixel of its canvas after drawing them, which makes the browser draw them then. In two walks at 100 % load falling to 100 and to 76 %, the desktop p95s were 11.6 and 10.5 ms (trace), 10.4 and 9.7 (print), 4.6 and 5.7 (cad) and 10.0 and 7.8 (fold), against the portal's 17.2 and 12.7 in the same walks, and on the slow reference 20.5 and 18.1, 14.1 and 16.1, 9.6 and 7.6, and 27.9 and 25.6.
- **Oil paint, laid and scraped.** [`oil.js`](../../site/pieces/oil.js) draws oil paint as lit height fields: a brush stroke (paint ridged along its sides, furrowed by the bristles, each bristle's streak a little lighter or darker, crested where the brush lifted, its dry end in strands, with a short cast shadow) and a knife's smear, grained along its length. Both are drawn once per solve and device scale into sprites, a body per colour and shape and a light per shape and per sixteenth of a turn against the light, so a stroke costs two image copies, and a third for a neighbour's paint it drags. [`impasto.js`](../../site/pieces/impasto.js) holds the [impasto](../../skills/artifex/styles/impasto.md) style's strokes scaled to the sheet, their colours mixed a quarter at a time across the style's thresholds; the order and pace the brush lays them; and the knife-spread ground, a lit height field per sweep with the papercraft's strips and parts raised through it, kept per seed and scale so the two shots share it. [`scrape.js`](../../site/pieces/scrape.js) places the [Kandinsky](../../skills/artifex/styles/kandinsky.md) style's forms on the bird, the composition widened to the frame, and the knife's steps: each step clears a region (a band, a sector or a strip) to the scraped ground, kept once with every stroke smeared along the pass that took it (the bird's only on the bird's place), and shows there the forms laid by then, in the style's order, the glazes multiplied; the background's passes and the halos go round the bird's strokes, and the paint the knife gathers is heaped along its edge. [`folded.js`](../../site/pieces/folded.js) draws the fold's picture for `fold.cjs`, so the paint seam begins on its last frame. The paint seam keeps the fold's last frame with each sweep of the ground as it is laid, then the strokes in copies of 0.4 s of brushing each; the Kandinsky shot keeps the scraped ground, and the impasto with the knife's steps over it five at a time. A browser clipping to a convex polygon of a few edges takes another path the first time it meets one, which cost 25 to 150 ms, so every region has at least 16 edges and each step is laid through a clip of its own. In two walks at 43 to 62 % load, the desktop p95s were 6.3 and 8.0 ms (paint) and 10.4 and 11.9 (kandinsky), and on the slow reference 22.7 and 23.1, and 12.7 and 12.1; their cold draws, made off screen as warm-ups, took 266 to 374 ms on desktop and 798 to 942 ms on the slow reference.

**Posters.** `ink`, `mirror`, `cad` and `kandinsky` carry posters in [`site/posters/`](../../site/posters/): the piece at the site's seed (1) and its still playhead (1), at its design size of 1200 by 800, written as WebP at quality 0.82 by installed Edge's `canvas.toBlob`. They are the reduced-motion stills of the four shots whose first draw costs hundreds of milliseconds. The intro has none: its still draws in a few milliseconds, and a poster of the first shot counts in the first load.

`npm run posters` ([`tools/posters.js`](../../tools/posters.js)) draws every poster the shot list names, in installed Edge through `withEdge`, and writes each one that changed to the path the shot names. Beside each it writes `<poster>.json`: the recipe (piece, seed, playhead, width, height, type, quality), a SHA-256 of every source file the piece reaches (its own modules and the library modules they require, line endings aside) and a SHA-256 of the poster's bytes. It is a command of its own, not a `npm run site` option, because the build needs no browser and runs in CI. `npm run site:check` fails a poster whose record is missing, whose recipe or sources differ from the shot list and sources as they stand, naming what moved, or whose file is not the one its record names; run `npm run posters` after changing a poster's piece or its shot. The check never redraws, because WebP bytes can differ between machines. Its hash covers the piece's sources, not the drawing runtime (`core/render.js`, `core/piece.js`) or the browser, so a change there needs `npm run posters` run by hand; drawn on this machine (Edge 153), the ink and mirror posters came out byte-identical to the files first written for them.

## What the build writes

`tools/build-site.js` loads every piece through `loadExternal` in [`tools/piece-input.js`](../../tools/piece-input.js) as one list. A list shares one module table: a helper two pieces require is defined once, and the result names the modules each piece reaches. Modules reached by the same two or more shots go together in one `shared-<n>.js`, numbered in the order of the first shot that reaches them; the rest go in `shot-<name>.js`. A shot's scripts in `data.js` are the shared scripts that hold modules it reaches, then its own, so a shot loads every module its piece reaches and no other, and the first load holds only what the first shot draws. With the current shot list the art shots up to the paint seam share a chain, each shot reaching everything the one before it shares and more, so `shared-1.js` is the paper and wordmark every art shot draws, and the paint seam loads thirteen shared scripts; the Kandinsky shot reaches the chain as far as the print's, and the papercut and oil paint it shares with the fold and the paint seam; the placeholders share their lattice in `shared-14.js`. Split this way, the first load went from 101.4 to 54.4 kB of brotli and the whole site from 282.8 to 293.6 kB, because each file is compressed on its own; with the paint seam and the Kandinsky shot the first load is 54.7 kB and the whole site 403.2 kB, the Kandinsky poster included. `core.js` is `bundle()` from [`tools/build-page.js`](../../tools/build-page.js) given a module list: the module runtime, the library modules in `core/` or `examples/` that the shots' modules require (`library` in the loader's result) and that `stage.js` and `worker.js` name in literal `req('…')` calls, every module those reach, and an `examples/index.js` registry of the shots' pieces alone. The list is derived at each build, never kept by hand; the page (`npm run page`) still bundles the whole library. A list that leaves out a module one of its own requires, or names a module that is not in the library, is refused by name before anything is written, and a shot whose require the loader cannot read, such as a computed path, is refused naming its file. `index.html` is `site/index.html` with the shot sections filled in and, at its `<!-- data -->` marker, a script tag for `data.js`, which sets `window.__siteData` to the shot data the stage reads: the shots, their scripts, tiers, posters and labels, the frame count, `hz` and the seed. The data is a same-origin script rather than text in the page, so the stage never reads markup or DOM text as data. `stage.js`, `worker.js` and `site.css` are copied. Every script is parsed before it is written, and `out/site/` is replaced whole.

The page carries a Content-Security-Policy of `default-src 'self'; img-src 'self' blob:`: no third-party request, no inline script. Without script, the text reads in order.

**The public boundary.** The build refuses any module, asset, shot list or site file whose real path lies under `.private/`, `docs/tasks/`, `docs/decisions/`, `docs/knowledge/private/` or `docs/apis/private/`, wherever those directories are, naming the file. Symbolic links are followed before the test.

**The preview server** serves only regular files under `out/site/` with a known type, on loopback, to `GET` and `HEAD`; any other path, including one that climbs out of the directory, is a 404.

## The stage

[`site/stage.js`](../../site/stage.js) maps `scrollY` to a whole frame of the shot list (0.6 viewport heights of scroll per second of film), finds the shot with `shotAt`, and maps the frame inside the shot to the piece's playhead, both ends included. It draws only when the piece's own frame, the seed, the parameters or the canvas size change.

- **Scroll is never taken.** The stage reads the scroll. It writes it only when the visitor starts "Play as film" and once when a recipe URL opens; every listener is passive, and nothing calls `preventDefault`.
- **Two canvases at most.** One holds the shot on screen. The other holds the neighbour in the direction of travel: its scripts load two seconds of film before the join and its first frame is drawn one second before, so the join shows a frame already drawn.
- **Scripts load as the visitor nears a shot**: every script the shot names, in parallel, and a script already loaded for an earlier shot is not fetched again. The shot draws only once all of them have run, whether the visitor scrolled to it, jumped to it by a recipe URL or reached its still under reduced motion; the worker imports the same scripts before its first draw of the shot.
- **Three tiers.** A timed piece draws per frame on the main thread; a still draws once per change; a worker-tier shot draws in a Worker on the stage canvas, handed over with `transferControlToOffscreen`, and its poster covers it until the first frame is ready. Where that route is missing or fails, a worker-tier shot draws on the main thread once the scroll has rested for 150 ms, and never warms.
- **Scale lowering.** A main-thread shot draws at three quarters of its raster scale when at least 3 of its last 20 judged draws take over `1000 / hz - 4` ms (29.3 ms at 30 Hz), down to one device pixel per CSS pixel, and then moves to the worker tier. A cold draw, the first after the piece loads, after a new solve or on a new canvas, size or scale, is not judged, nor is a warm-up draw: a piece makes its kept copies and first marks once, and that is not what each frame costs. The draw time measured here is the time of the drawing calls, which is not the rasterization time, except with `?measure=1`, where each draw is forced to rasterize before the clock is read (see [what the checks establish](#what-the-checks-establish)).
- **Canvas size.** The piece's design box fits inside the viewport, centred, at the device pixel ratio, capped at 8,294,400 pixels.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`): nothing draws on scroll. Each shot that is not a seam shows one still, its poster or its `still` playhead drawn once as an image, as its figure nears the viewport. The preference can change without a reload. "Play as film" still plays, as an explicit request, and returns to the stills when stopped.
- **Recipes.** The address bar follows the frame on screen once the scroll has rested for 400 ms: `?shot=<name>&seed=<n>&t=<playhead>&p.<name>=<value>`, where `t` is the piece's quantized playhead, as the page writes it. Opening such a URL scrolls once to that frame. A parameter the piece refuses is dropped. "Copy link" copies it; "Re-roll seed" moves to the next seed.
- **Play as film** scrolls the site at `hz` frames a second from where it stands. The button pauses it; any key, pointer press, wheel or touch elsewhere stops it.

`window.__stage` reports the mode, the frame and shot, every draw (`log`, with its shot, frame, time, tier, scale, canvas width, whether it warmed a neighbour and whether it was cold), the stills drawn, the scroll writes and why, scale lowering and the worker's state, and carries the stage's `force`, which the check calibrates. The check reads it.

## What the checks establish

`npm run site:check` builds the real shot list and fixtures into temporary directories and checks: every module defined once, each shot's scripts defining exactly the modules its piece reaches, modules the same shots share in one shared script and each shot's own module in its script, and the first load counting the first shot's scripts alone; every piece loading and validating from `core.js` and its scripts in a VM; `core.js` holding no example, a registry naming every shot, the modules the stage requires, a library module a shot requires through a helper and none that nothing requires; a computed require refused naming its file; the sections, seams and stills in the page; the page's only scripts being `data.js`, `core.js` and `stage.js`, and `data.js` holding the build's data; the first load within 150 kB of brotli; a poster named in the shot list as a `javascript:`, `data:` or other-origin URL refused as a missing file, and one outside the shot list's directory published only as `posters/<name>.<ext>`; each shot-list refusal; the private-path refusal for modules, entries, assets and the shot list; escaped copy; the preview server's refusals; the kept copies' release rule (`site/tests/held.test.js`: three solves kept, the oldest emptied by a fourth and remade when drawn again, the third given up over the ceiling but never the two last drawn, counted copies and their spares emptied with their solve, and a replaced copy emptied); the folded reflections' pixels, hashed on stand-in canvases, against those of the fold before its smooth path was written out (`site/tests/inversion.test.js`); and the stage's frame mapping, recipe round trip, scale lowering (a cold draw and two spikes in twenty do not lower, three over-budget draws do, and the draw after a scale change is not judged) and canvas fit; the check's count of redraws after a scale change; every poster's record against its shot's current recipe and sources, and the staleness rule on a fixture (`site/tests/posters.test.js`: a changed helper, seed, playhead or size, a replaced poster and a missing record or poster fail, line endings alone do not); and its sink calibration verdict (a forced time near the whole-frame copy passes, one near the unforced calls, under half the copy, or too light to tell fails). The calibration's times need installed Edge. [`tests/external-piece.test.js`](../../tests/external-piece.test.js) checks the loader's list form, and three mutations in `tests/negative.js` guard it; `tests/examples.test.js` checks that the default bundle is the whole library in order and that a module list with a hole, or a module outside the library, is refused by name.

`tools/check-site.js` runs in installed Edge through `withEdge` from [`tools/check-browser.js`](../../tools/check-browser.js), with its temporary profile and cleanup, against the builder's loopback server. A probe installed before the page's first script counts scroll writes, `preventDefault` on wheel, touch, scroll and key events, and listeners for them that are not passive. On two references, desktop (1280 by 800 at DPR 1) and slow (390 by 844 at DPR 3, a 1170-pixel canvas, 4x CPU throttling), it scrolls the whole film forward by native wheel events, one frame of scroll per event, drawing forced to rasterize (`?measure=1`): after each draw the stage reads one pixel of it through a new 1-by-1 GPU-backed canvas, which cannot answer before the frame is rasterized, and only then reads the clock. The p95 is therefore the drawing calls plus their rasterization, forced through a one-pixel GPU readback. Until this rule the sink was a 1-by-1 canvas kept for reading (`willReadFrequently`), and drawing into it copied the whole stage canvas to the CPU: in installed Edge that copy cost 4.4 ms a frame at 1200 by 800 and 10 ms at 2400 by 1600, against 1.8 to 2.2 ms for the GPU sink, a cost no visitor pays. Figures measured before this change are not comparable with those after it; the budgets are unchanged. Before walking, each reference calibrates the sink on a canvas the stage's size: a frame of few drawing calls and heavy rasterization (one 4,000-point stroke) is timed six times unforced, six forced by the stage's `force` and six forced by the old whole-frame copy, alternated, and the walk fails unless the forced median is at least half the copied one and three times the unforced one, so a sink that stopped waiting fails instead of letting every shot pass fast (`sink` in the JSON). The walk then requires: no page errors; three wheel steps too small for a new frame draw nothing; the walk ends on the last frame; every shot drawn; as many draws as distinct frames, besides one redraw of the frame on screen after each scale change, counted apart; at most two canvases; no scroll write, `preventDefault` or active listener; and each main-thread shot's p95 draw time, at the scale it settled on and over its draws after the cold one, within 12 ms (desktop) or 33 ms (slow) per frame, or 100 ms and 300 ms for a still. The cold draws are left out of the p95 but reported, with their times, in the JSON, so a stall once per page stays visible. Each walk also reports, as `kept` in the JSON and in the summary line, the copies `held.js` keeps on the main thread at its end and the most megapixels they held at once, from the tally it publishes as `window.__held`. Then reduced motion must show a loaded still with alternative text for every shot that is not a seam, hide the stage and the seams, draw nothing on scroll, and return to the film when the preference changes back; with `transferControlToOffscreen` removed, the worker-tier shot must draw on the main thread; a recipe URL must open its shot, frame and seed with exactly one scroll write, and rewrite the address bar; and "Play as film" must reach within a few frames of `hz` frames after one second, and stop on a key.

Measured on this repository's development machine (Edge 153, headless), which other work was loading at 36 to 100 % CPU during the runs (load at a run's start and end, from Node's CPU times), so the figures move by several milliseconds between runs. With the GPU sink, over three runs (100 to 36 %, 100 to 98 %, 70 to 37 %), the desktop p95s were 4.0 to 5.6 ms (intro), 5.1 to 6.7 (bloom), 9.1 to 10.5 (ink), 8.4 to 10.7 (crack), 6.6 to 8.1 (mirror), 9.8 to 11.4 (bend) and 12.5 to 15.7 (portal), and on the slow reference 10.6 to 13.6, 15.4 to 21.3, 16.6 to 21.3, 14.7 to 25.3, 11.1 to 17.5, 19.8 to 29.1 and 23.1 to 28.6, where the stage lowered the scale of portal, and in two runs of bend, in every run. Every shot but the portal held its budget in all three; the portal went over 12 ms on desktop in all three. **Kept copies and the portal.** Walking the whole film on desktop with copies kept for every shot, `held.js` ended holding 40 canvases of 31.7 megapixels, and Edge's memory dump gave its GPU process 89 MB of shared images (canvas backings); with the release rule it ends with 17 canvases of 9.6 megapixels, peaks at 22.5, and the GPU process holds 65 MB. Scrolling back from the portal into crack and ink, whose copies had been given up, remade them on the first draw back, the warm-up drawn off screen a second before the join: 94 ms for crack and 47 ms for ink, against steady medians of 6.4 and 5.6 ms. **Where the portal's time goes.** Timed with the GPU sink on its own 1200-by-800 canvas, every frame, alternated with variants that leave one part out (at 44 to 100 % load), the portal's slowest frames are its last two seconds, where the camera is deep in the mirror, and they are bound by the GPU, not the CPU: a median of about 9 ms of which about 2 ms is JavaScript. Leaving out the page drawn through the inversion there brings that median to about 4.6 ms and its p95 from about 13 to about 7; of the page, its ruled lines, drawn as arcs, cost about 2 ms, its pen lines 1.6 and its fills 0.9. The CPU fold barely moves the p95: a coarser fold grid, tried for timing only, cut its time from 3.1 to 1.8 ms and left the p95 at 12.8. Leaving out, from the paths, arcs and rim segments that lie outside the frame saved nothing measurable and changed the antialiasing of what stays in it, up to 16 levels on a few hundred pixels, because Edge rasterizes a path as a whole. The fold's smooth path now reads its three channels written out instead of through a function made per pixel, a quarter faster on the frames that use it, with pixels identical to before (`site/tests/inversion.test.js`, and a frame-by-frame comparison of the portal and bend at scales 1 and 0.75 in Edge). The portal stays over the 12 ms desktop budget under load: in walks on the same machine at 83 to 100 % load, 15.3 and 12.4 ms before this change and 13.7, 13.2 and 13.1 after (the first four alternated). It has not been measured on a quiet machine.

Under the rule before the one above (a p95 of the last 20 draws, the second-highest, cold draws included), the stage lowered bend and portal on the slow reference in every run, and the check counted each redraw after a lowering as a duplicate, so its draw count failed in every such run. Under the present rule, in two runs at 74 to 100 % load, the slow walks counted 7 and 4 redraws after scale changes apart and held the draw count; bend and portal were still lowered, on their steady draws.
