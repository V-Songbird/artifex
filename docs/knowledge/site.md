# Showcase site

The site is one film the visitor scrolls. Every section, and every seam between two sections, is a shot played by an ordinary Artifex piece; the scroll position is the playhead of the whole site. Scrolling back rewinds, because every frame is a function of the seed and the playhead.

The site's twenty-two shots are its art (see [the art shots](#the-art-shots)). The last two, the seam `close` and the shot `home`, go on from the box shot at the same box, in the worker: the backing paper slides out from under the frame and folds over it into an envelope, and the plotter's pen writes the recipe, the seed and the box, on its label ([`envelope.js`](../../site/pieces/envelope.js); the box shot's own drawing is in [`boxed.js`](../../site/pieces/boxed.js), which all three share).

## Commands

| Command | Purpose and result |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run site` | Builds `out/site/` from `site/shots.js` and prints the brotli size of the first load and of the whole site. |
| `npm run site -- --serve [--port N]` | Builds, then serves `out/site/` on `127.0.0.1` until stopped. |
| `npm run site:check` | Runs `site/tests/*.test.js`: the builder, the refusals, the preview server, the stage's pure helpers (sound's included), the kept copies' release rule, the folded reflections' pixels, the fall and the skins the shot-4 pieces share, the site check's pure helpers, the exports script, the style demos' own promises and whether every poster was drawn from its shot's current recipe. |
| `npm run posters [-- --edge PATH] [--timeout-ms N] [--headed]` | Draws every poster the shot list names in installed Edge and writes each that changed, with its recipe record beside it (see [posters](#posters)). |
| `node tools/check-site.js [--edge PATH] [--timeout-ms N] [--headed]` | Checks the built site in installed Edge; run `npm run site` first. A state the page does not reach within two minutes stops the run with the phase and the expression waited for, and the phases already finished are still printed. |

The site's tests are not in `tests/*.test.js`, so the mutation suite, which runs only files in `tests/`, does not grow with the site. The [Check workflow](../../.github/workflows/check.yml) builds the site and runs `npm run site:check`; it deploys nothing and does not run the Edge check. The [Site workflow](../../.github/workflows/site.yml) starts only by hand and deploys nothing: one job runs `node tools/check-site.js` on a hosted Windows runner, where a frame budget over its limit does not stop the run because the runner draws in software; the other builds the site and runs `npm run site:check`.

## The shot list

`site/shots.js` exports `{ hz, seed, shots }`. `hz` is the site's frame rate: frames of scroll per second of film. `seed` is the seed every piece is solved with, so a seam and the shots on either side of it agree. Each shot has:

| Key | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `name` | Kebab-case and unique; recipe URLs and script names use it. |
| `piece` | Path to a CommonJS piece, from `site/shots.js`. |
| `seam` | A transformation between two shots: hidden from assistive technology, and not shown under reduced motion. |
| `seconds` | How long a still holds. Required for a still; refused for a timed piece, which lasts its own duration. |
| `heard` | Path to the same piece with its sound, loaded apart from the shot the first time its sound is wanted (see [what the build writes](#what-the-build-writes)). |
| `tier` | `'worker'` draws the shot off the main thread; otherwise a timed piece draws per frame and a still once per change. |
| `still` | The playhead shown under reduced motion; 1 by default. |
| `poster` | Path to a `.webp`, `.png`, `.jpg` or `.jpeg` drawn from the same recipe, from `site/shots.js`, shown until a worker-tier frame is ready and used as the reduced-motion still. The build copies it to `posters/<name>.<ext>`, and the page names only that path. |
| `title`, `text` | The copy over the shot, as HTML-escaped text. |

Unknown keys are refused. The list is resolved with `shots` from [`core/time.js`](../../core/time.js) at build time, so a shot too short to hold a whole frame fails the build.

### How the art shots are built

- **The forms come loose and play.** [`loose.js`](../../site/pieces/loose.js) takes each solid form the knife left (every one but the three halos) off the painting as a skin of dry paint. A build stage records what each form's drawing paints on a surface that only follows the transform and the path, arcs swept as a canvas sweeps them: its bounds, its middle, its size, its kind of shape (round, a line, or flat, which sets how it sounds and the body it falls as), the forms lying over it and the one it lies on. On a canvas it keeps, per seed, parameters and scale, the painting with no forms and a faint stain where each lay, each skin as a sprite on the painting's own pixel grid (a glaze carries the ground under it away with it), and a soft shadow and a cut edge per skin, so a frame lays copies.

  [`score.js`](../../site/pieces/score.js) is one cue table in seconds from the unstick seam's start: the order the forms come free, a form only once every form over it is free, then every slide, hop, shake and sound of the staff, the band, its phrase, the reading of pi's digits and the chord. A slide lasts as long as its way and its weight make it, so no two sound alike. Each of the five pieces draws the table at its own seconds from the same build, which reuses the wall's, its `frame them` stage from `hang.js` included, so the works keep the sizes the wall drew them at; each piece starts on the last frame of the one before it, and `part` in `score.js` gives each piece its start and duration from one list of the five durations.

  At 1200 pixels, seeds 1 to 3, every join from the wall to the contain seam differs by 0 levels on average and 0.3 at most. Under load, the five pieces' desktop p95s measured 5.9 to 10.5 ms and on the slow reference 12.9 to 18.3. Each piece is a solve of its own, but the five draw the same forms from the same build, so `owner` in `loose.js` keeps the copies, the painting's and the hang's under the first solve seen of a seed and its parameters, the last three kept: a piece reached after another lays the copies the other made. Their cold draws are made off screen as warm-ups.

  In two alternated site checks, unstick's, the first of the five, took 554 to 611 ms on desktop and 1,032 to 1,372 ms on the slow reference; cues' and readout's took 29 to 36 ms and 110 to 153 ms; settle's and contain's, which also solve or extend the fall, 181 to 201 ms and 665 to 916 ms. Drawn in Edge at 1200 and 1170 pixels wide, each piece alone and the five in order either way, every frame compared was identical.
- **A fall solved once.** [`fall.js`](../../site/pieces/fall.js) solves the fall in the build, four substeps a frame from the release: each form is a rigid body of capsules that follow its paint (a disc one circle, a single stroke a chain along it, any other form its outline and a core), the rainbow's arcs one body and the long arc left where it lies. A trembling first shakes apart the forms lying over one another; then they let go in order, notes, staff lines and band, and fall under gravity, with contacts seen a little ahead of touching, impulses with friction, a gentle push out of any overlap, and sleep once still. The contain seam's sides are walls of the same fall, closing in as it draws them. A frame reads the kept track. A fall is kept per seed, the last three, so contain goes on from settle's fall instead of solving it again, and settle reached after contain reads its fall from contain's: either way the track and the strikes equal a cold solve (`site/tests/fall.test.js`). Solved alone in Edge, settle's build took 151 to 315 ms and contain's 311 to 473 ms, seeds 1 to 9 (`npm run seeds -- ./site/pieces/<piece>.cjs 9`).
- **Any box.** [`box.cjs`](../../site/pieces/box.cjs) is the site's only piece that declares `boxes` (see [responsive boxes](../apis/piece-api.md#responsive-boxes)), from 400 to 2400 design units wide and 400 to 2800 tall; the stage solves it for the box it shows (see [the stage](#the-stage)). Its first frame at any box is the contain seam's last frame, centred at the same size, with the page's ground round it where the box is larger, so the join shows no change. Everything before the box moves depends only on the seed and the parameters, so its first build stage keeps that build and the contain seam's fall for the last three recipes and shares them; its second, `fit the box`, goes on with the fall from where the contain seam kept it (`onward` in [`fall.js`](../../site/pieces/fall.js)), the side walls moving to the box, and keeps that fall for the last eight boxes. In Node, the shared build took about 560 ms cold, and each new box about 200 to 240 ms, most of it the fall; a box seen before takes under a millisecond. Its drawing is the score's, translated by whole multiples of four device pixels so the painting's kept copy is the contain seam's own; the hang's view (`view` in [`hang.js`](../../site/pieces/hang.js)) takes the box's half size, so neighbouring works appear where a wide or tall box reaches them.
### Sound and ambience

- **Sound, from the same table.** [`voices.js`](../../site/pieces/voices.js) turns the table's events into voices of [`core/sound.js`](../../core/sound.js) in the room of a table: a crackle of grains as a form cracks free, a soft tuned note as it lands, grains of friction along each slide, thick while it moves fast and coloured by its shape and size, low strings as the staff's lines are laid and struck, drums, mallets and a bell for the band, a knock and ticks, each digit's note, each strike of the fall as hard as it struck, creaks and a hollow ring for the box, and a held chord as the pile rests. The fall's strikes are thinned so a pile is not a wall of noise: only strikes faster than 150 sheet units a second are heard, each form at most every quarter second, and while the sides push, only those faster than 180, and softer. Each piece declares its own `sound` and plays the events that fall in its own seconds, the contain seam up to its last frame, which is the box shot's first; notes begun up to six seconds before it, longer than any voice lasts, go on ringing from where they had decayed, and a slide under way goes on scraping, so consecutive soundtracks join. The box shot adds the sides' creak as they move to the box, a hollow ring as they stop and the chord, and fades out over its last quarter second, because the shot after it is silent. The site plays them when the visitor presses **Listen** (see [the stage](#the-stage)).
- **Each seam sounds its own event.** The eight seams before shot 4 (`bloom`, `crack`, `bend`, `portal`, `trace`, `print`, `fold` and `paint`) each declare `sound`, placed from the seam's own state with the helpers in [`sounds.js`](../../site/pieces/sounds.js): `bloom` the wide brush's wet swish left to right with the water's crackle at its tips, and each plume of ink letting go as a dark swell of water, as full as its load; `crack` the drying sheet crackling and each tear a rip, its fibres giving in a crackle from its flaw over the rasp of the sheet parting, the first generations long and dark, the later ones short and further off; `bend` each mirror's glass flexing in a soft rush and creaking in its pins as it bows, and the kaleidoscope's ratchet clicking faster as the turn gathers; `portal` the ratchet slowing as the turn dies away, the page's dry hiss as it slides in, and the air of the glass opening round the camera as it goes into the mirror; `trace` the mirror springing flat with a dull flex and creaks, the film's plastic hiss, the instruments knocked down and the pen's tap and dry scratch along every line, each line pressed a little differently; `print` the page pulled out, the lamp's switch on and off and its housing ticking as it cools, the film peeled away in a fine crackle and the water running across the sheet with bubbles breaking in it; `fold` the gantry rolling home, the knife scoring every cut, the parts lifting, the crease, the two tears left to right as a crackle over the paper's rasp, and the strips set down; `paint` the knife's three wet sweeps and the brush's dabs, each of the first heard, then a patter that swells and eases patch by patch.

  The seven seams before the paint seam sound their materials as noise shaped in time, not as pitches: a snap (a band of noise a few milliseconds long: a fibre, a nib, a click), a knock (a snap over a body that dies within a tenth of a second), a breath, and a swell (one stream of noise that grows and ebbs, its level wavering between seeded breaths every 40 ms and its band moving every 0.1 s, so air and water never beat as a tremolo). A drop or a bubble is a wet tick over a short rising chirp. No pitch rings longer than half a second in their soundtracks or ambiences (`site/tests/sounds.test.js`), because a held or repeated sine is heard as a device. `place` in `sounds.js` builds a snap as voice's transient alone (`tick`) and a swell from two seconds of seeded noise shared per context (`gust`), because the stage builds a soundtrack's graph on the main thread while the film plays: built that way, each of the seven soundtracks builds in 27 to 50 ms in Node, several times faster than as overlapping voices. Whether their films replay is under "Seam films and replay" in [what `tools/check-site.js` checks in Edge](#what-toolscheck-sitejs-checks-in-edge). The paint seam still voices its contacts as a few inharmonic sine partials (`grain` in `sounds.js`). Each seam's soundtrack fades out over its last quarter second, since the shot after it may be silent. The shots `intro`, `ink`, `mirror`, `cad`, `kandinsky` and `wall` declare no sound yet.
- **Each early seam has an ambience.** The same seven seams carry an ambience on their `sound` function, `sound.ambience(ctx, state, { loop })`, made by `ambience` in `sounds.js`: a few seeded quiet events of the seam's material, at many levels, places and distances, in a room and over no noise bed. `bloom` has the wet sheet's crackle, drops and slow dark swells; `crack` the dry torn sheet settling; `bend` the bowed glass creaking and glinting; `portal` the kaleidoscope's ratchet barely turning, glints and air in the tube; `trace` the film stirring and a pen far off; `print` bubbles, drips and trickles; `fold` paper rustling and settling. Events come at chance gaps (`scatter`), so they fall alone or in small runs, and their level rises and falls once over the loop. The stage plays an ambience while its shot is scrolled (see [the stage](#the-stage)); the piece contract is unchanged, because the ambience is a property of the `sound` function, which `validate` keeps as it is.
### Drawing, kept copies and the hang

- **One ink.** [`paper.js`](../../site/pieces/paper.js) holds the paper and one ink: `tone(c)` is the colour ink at concentration `c` leaves on the paper, and `wash(c)` the ink at the opacity that leaves it with ordinary source-over drawing. The multiply composite doubled a frame's cost on a GPU-backed canvas.
- **What stays is kept.** [`held.js`](../../site/pieces/held.js) keeps what a shot has finished drawing as copies per canvas, scale and solve: `hold` and `keep` for an opaque layer, `sized` for a canvas of a size of its own, `upTo` for marks laid one after another (a copy every fourth count and two recently used ones), `soft` for marks laid at a fraction of the resolution and smoothed back. A copy is made on the shot's first frame; when the stage lowers the scale, the copies are shrunk rather than redrawn. Copies are kept only for the three solves drawn last (on the site, the shot on screen, the one warmed beside it and the one before), and the oldest of the three only while all copies together hold at most 24 megapixels; a solve given up has its canvases emptied at once, and its copies are made again, as on its first frame, if it is drawn again. A desktop walk of the whole film ends holding 182 copies of 17.7 megapixels, most of them shot 4's skins, which its five pieces share, and peaks at 22.5 (see [what the checks establish](#what-the-checks-establish)). Its `prime` hook draws, on the shot's own canvas under the first copy, every kind of mark the shot will make: a GPU-backed canvas costs 25 to 40 ms the first time it meets a kind of mark, and the first frame is drawn before the shot is on screen.
- **Curved mirrors, pixel by pixel.** A circle mirror reflects by inversion, which no copy of a picture can show, so [`inversion.js`](../../site/pieces/inversion.js) folds every pixel outside the region between the mirrors back into it on the CPU, at one folded pixel per 3.6 canvas pixels each way, reading the lifted scene's colours once at half the design's resolution, a little darker for every reflection and blended to the mean tone past seven. The reflections in `bend` and `portal` are therefore softer than the sheet between the mirrors; the page the camera goes into in `portal` is drawn sharp, its lines mapped through the inversion (straight lines as exact arcs). The display uses neither WebGL nor WebGPU.
- **The page** ([`page.js`](../../site/pieces/page.js)) is built from the site's demo subject ([`site/demos/subject.js`](../../site/demos/subject.js)) and the style library's helpers ([`kit.js`](../../skills/artifex/styles/kit.js)) as the [doodle](../../skills/artifex/styles/doodle.md) style draws it, as polylines, so it can be drawn through any map.
- **Where the portal ends.** [`enter.js`](../../site/pieces/enter.js) draws the portal's picture for `portal.cjs` and gives the shots after it the portal's camera (`camera`) and its last view with the mirror let go (`released`): the page drawn through a blend of the inversion and a similarity fitted to the doodled bird, so the trace begins on the portal's last frame and ends on a flat page the square tracing lies on exactly.
- **One sheet, one subject, four media.** [`draft.js`](../../site/pieces/draft.js) lays out the drafting sheet in its own units (a landscape CAD sheet, the front view where the doodled bird lies) with the film, the ink's schedule and the instruments; [`blueprint.js`](../../site/pieces/blueprint.js) the cyanotype print of that film (a brushed coat, its exposure and wash); [`cadsheet.js`](../../site/pieces/cadsheet.js) the rest of the CAD drawing, following the [CAD](../../skills/artifex/styles/cad.md) style's layout, layers and dimensions, as marks in the order a plotter draws them; [`papercut.js`](../../site/pieces/papercut.js) the cut, the layers, the fold and the tears, following the [papercraft](../../skills/artifex/styles/papercraft.md) style's torn cores, clean cut edges and shadows. The CAD style's dark ground and light lines come from the print: the inked film shades a cyanotype sheet, which washes Prussian blue with white lines, on paper that can then be cut and folded, its white back and core the papercraft's cream. Every straight line of the tracing is ruled against its instrument.

  The sheet is kept rather than drawn each frame: the print keeps it dry, exposed and washed, at its last view, which holds every earlier one, and mixes them (the washed copy kept only behind the water, by `destination-in`); the CAD shot keeps it at its first view and its last, laying the close copy over the whole one while it draws back, and adds each mark the pen finishes to a kept copy of the sheet; the fold keeps the plotted sheet at its first view and its close one. The notebook page the print pulls away is drawn only where it is in the frame, and nothing under it while it covers the frame. A browser drops what an opaque copy then covers whole, the fold's first-frame marks included, so the fold copies a pixel of its canvas after drawing them, which makes the browser draw them then. At full load the four shots' desktop p95s measured 4.6 to 11.6 ms, close to the 12 ms budget, and on the slow reference 7.6 to 27.9.
- **Oil paint, laid and scraped.** [`oil.js`](../../site/pieces/oil.js) draws oil paint as lit height fields: a brush stroke (paint ridged along its sides, furrowed by the bristles, each bristle's streak a little lighter or darker, crested where the brush lifted, its dry end in strands, with a short cast shadow) and a knife's smear, grained along its length. Both are drawn once per solve and device scale into sprites, a body per colour and shape and a light per shape and per sixteenth of a turn against the light, so a stroke costs two image copies, and a third for a neighbour's paint it drags.

  [`impasto.js`](../../site/pieces/impasto.js) holds the [impasto](../../skills/artifex/styles/impasto.md) style's strokes scaled to the sheet, their colours mixed a quarter at a time across the style's thresholds; the order and pace the brush lays them; and the knife-spread ground, a lit height field per sweep with the papercraft's strips and parts raised through it, kept per seed and scale so the two shots share it. [`scrape.js`](../../site/pieces/scrape.js) places the [Kandinsky](../../skills/artifex/styles/kandinsky.md) style's forms on the bird, the composition widened to the frame, and the knife's steps: each step clears a region (a band, a sector or a strip) to the scraped ground, kept once with every stroke smeared along the pass that took it (the bird's only on the bird's place), and shows there the forms laid by then, in the style's order, the glazes multiplied; the background's passes and the halos go round the bird's strokes, and the paint the knife gathers is heaped along its edge. [`folded.js`](../../site/pieces/folded.js) draws the fold's picture for `fold.cjs`, so the paint seam begins on its last frame.

  The paint seam keeps the fold's last frame with each sweep of the ground as it is laid, then the strokes in copies of 0.4 s of brushing each; the Kandinsky shot keeps the scraped ground, and the impasto with the knife's steps over it five at a time. A browser clipping to a convex polygon of a few edges takes another path the first time it meets one, which cost 25 to 150 ms, so every region has at least 16 edges and each step is laid through a clip of its own. At moderate load the paint seam's desktop p95 measured 6.3 to 8.0 ms and the Kandinsky shot's 10.4 to 11.9, and on the slow reference 22.7 to 23.1 and 12.1 to 12.7; their cold draws, made off screen as warm-ups, took up to 374 ms on desktop and 942 ms on the slow reference.
- **Every style, one hang.** [`hang.js`](../../site/pieces/hang.js) lays out one work of each of sixteen built-in styles, every style in [`builtin.json`](../../skills/artifex/styles/builtin.json) but `manga`, `gouache`, `flat-cartoon`, `print-plate` and `backlit-silhouette`, in the order `HUNG` names and four to a row, each matted and framed alike in the ink: the Kandinsky the knife scraped, on the whole sheet it was painted on, and each other style's demo in [`site/demos/`](../../site/demos/subject.js), the bird staged with that style's marks, at the site's seed, so another seed redraws the whole wall. [`wall.cjs`](../../site/pieces/wall.cjs) draws back to that sheet on the table, lowers the Kandinsky's frame over it from above, draws back to the whole hang and moves back onto the Kandinsky until its frame fills the view. The camera is `camera` in `hang.js`, which the unstick seam goes on from. A build stage, `frames` in `hang.js`, walks the camera and records the largest size at which it shows at least half of each work, and each work is drawn once at that size, on a canvas `sized` in `held.js` keeps with its solve. A style demo takes from a few milliseconds to a few hundred (the 3D render traces a ray per pixel), so once the wall is on screen the works are made ahead, one per idle callback in the order the camera reaches them, each read back a pixel so the browser draws it then; a frame that needs a work not yet made draws it at once, the same drawing at the same size.

  The Kandinsky is kept at the knife's camera, offset by whole multiples of four pixels so the ground's quarter-resolution stain falls on the same grid: the wall's first frame is the Kandinsky shot's last to within 0.03 levels on average at 1200 pixels, seeds 1 to 3. A frame's shadow is one blurred square laid in nine parts, so its fall-off keeps its width under a frame of any size. With sixteen works hung four to a row, the wall's desktop p95 measured 4.7 and 4.8 ms in two walks and on the slow reference 9.9 and 10.3; its cold draw, made off screen as a warm-up, took 296 and 397 ms on desktop and 1,282 and 1,240 ms on the slow reference, at about 45% CPU load. The pencil fill of the pen-and-pencil work strokes its pieces of one weight and width as one path: twelve fills at 1200 by 900 drew in 137 ms where a stroke per piece took 345. The Kandinsky shot before it goes over 12 ms on desktop under load, with the wall or without it; its slowest draws fall at the knife's steps, seconds 3 to 10, not in its last second, where the wall warms.

### Posters

`ink`, `mirror`, `cad`, `kandinsky`, `wall`, `cues`, `readout` and `settle` carry posters in [`site/posters/`](../../site/posters/): the piece at the site's seed (1) and its still playhead (1, or for the wall 0.6, the whole hang), at its design size of 1200 by 800, written as WebP at quality 0.82 by installed Edge's `canvas.toBlob`. They are the reduced-motion stills of the eight shots whose first draw costs hundreds of milliseconds. The intro has none: its still draws in a few milliseconds, and a poster of the first shot counts in the first load. Each poster's record hashes the modules its piece really loads. The `cad` shot is a piece of its own, drawn by [`cadsheet.js`](../../site/pieces/cadsheet.js) without the cad style's mark functions, so its poster follows the sheet and not [`cad.js`](../../skills/artifex/styles/cad.js); the wall hangs the style's own work, so the wall's poster follows the style.

`npm run posters` ([`tools/posters.js`](../../tools/posters.js)) draws every poster the shot list names, in installed Edge through `withEdge`, and writes each one that changed to the path the shot names. Beside each it writes `<poster>.json`: the recipe (piece, seed, playhead, width, height, type, quality), a SHA-256 of every source file the piece reaches (its own modules and the library modules they require, line endings, comments and a leading byte order mark (BOM) aside; U+2028 and U+2029 count as line endings in code and comments, and inside a string or template they hash as written) and a SHA-256 of the poster's bytes. It is a command of its own, not a `npm run site` option, because the build needs no browser and runs in CI. `npm run site:check` fails a poster whose record is missing, whose recipe or sources differ from the shot list and sources as they stand, naming what moved, or whose file is not the one its record names; run `npm run posters` after changing a poster's piece or its shot. The check never redraws, because WebP bytes can differ between machines. Its hash covers the piece's sources, not the drawing runtime (`core/render.js`, `core/piece.js`) or the browser, so a change there needs `npm run posters` run by hand. Drawn again in installed Edge 153, the ink and mirror posters came out byte-identical to the files first written for them.

## What the build writes

`tools/build-site.js` loads every piece through `loadExternal` in [`tools/piece-input.js`](../../tools/piece-input.js) as one list. A list shares one module table: a helper two pieces require is defined once, and the result names the modules each piece reaches. Modules reached by the same two or more shots go together in one `shared-<n>.js`, numbered in the order of the first shot that reaches them; the rest go in `shot-<name>.js`. A shot's scripts in `data.js` are the shared scripts that hold modules it reaches, then its own, so a shot loads every module its piece reaches and no other, and the first load holds only what the first shot draws.

A shot may name a `heard` piece: the same piece with its `sound`, in a file of its own. It joins the list after the shots, so the shot's scripts hold nothing its sound needs. `data.js` gives the shot `heard: { id, scripts }`, the shared scripts the heard piece reaches beyond the shot's own and then `heard-<name>.js`. The stage loads them the first time Listen is on for that shot, or a file of it is asked for, and from then the shot's piece carries the sound. The intro is the one shot that has one, [`intro-heard.cjs`](../../site/pieces/intro-heard.cjs): `sounds.js` and `core/sound.js` would otherwise add 18.7 kB to the first load.

With the current shot list:

- the art shots up to the paint seam share a chain of drawing modules, each shot reaching those the one before it draws with and more, so `shared-1.js` is the paper and wordmark every art shot draws, and the paint seam loads fifteen shared scripts;
- the eight seams with sound also share `sounds.js` in `shared-3.js`, and they, the five pieces of shot 4 and the box shot share `core/sound.js` in `shared-4.js`;
- the Kandinsky shot reaches the drawing chain as far as the print's, and the papercut and oil paint it shares with the fold and the paint seam;
- the wall reaches everything the Kandinsky shot does, its scraping included, and the fifteen style demos it frames, with the style marks they draw with;
- the five pieces of shot 4 and the box shot reach everything the wall does but the wall's own piece, so the hang and the style demos are in `shared-17.js` and `wall.cjs` in `shot-wall.js`;
- those six share the loose skins, the score and its voices in `shared-18.js`, and settle, contain and the box shot share the fall in `shared-19.js`;
- the box shot, the close seam and the home shot share the box's drawing, and the last two the envelope.

Splitting keeps the first load small and makes the whole site a little larger, because each file is compressed on its own: the product page's first load is 104.2 kB of brotli, the film's 113.7 kB, and the whole site 926.0 kB, its posters and the exports included.

`core.js` is `bundle()` from [`tools/build-page.js`](../../tools/build-page.js) given a module list: the module runtime, the library modules in `core/` or `examples/` that `stage.js` and `worker.js` name in literal `req('…')` calls and that the first shot's modules require, every module those reach, and an `examples/index.js` registry of the shots' pieces alone. A library module that only later shots reach is split like the shots' own modules, into the shared script or the shot's script of the shots that reach it: `core/field.js`, which every art shot after the intro reaches, is in `shared-2.js`, and `core/sound.js` in `shared-4.js`. The list is derived at each build, never kept by hand; the page (`npm run page`) still bundles the whole library.

`exports.js` holds [`core/export.js`](../../core/export.js), the exports the page and the site share, and every library module it reaches that `core.js` does not hold, such as `core/film.js`, without the module runtime; `data.js` names it and its module as `exports`. A library module that a shot's script also holds stays in both, because either script may load first; both copies are the same text, and the runtime keeps the first one it runs, so the second costs bytes only. It is not part of the first load: the stage fetches it the first time a visitor asks for a file.

A list that leaves out a module one of its own requires, or names a module that is not in the library, is refused by name before anything is written, and a shot whose require the loader cannot read, such as a computed path, is refused naming its file.

`film.html` is `site/film.html` with the shot sections filled in and, at its `<!-- data -->` marker, a script tag for `data.js`, which sets `window.__siteData` to the shot data the stage reads: the shots, their scripts, tiers, posters and labels, the frame count, `hz` and the seed. A shot whose piece declares boxes also carries its design size and box ranges, which the site check's box path reads. The data is a same-origin script rather than text in the page, so the stage never reads markup or DOM text as data. `stage.js`, `worker.js` and `site.css` are copied. Every script is parsed before it is written, and `out/site/` is replaced whole.

The page carries a Content-Security-Policy of `default-src 'self'; img-src 'self' blob:`: no third-party request, no inline script. Without script, the text reads in order.

**The public boundary.** The build refuses any module, asset, shot list or site file whose real path lies under `.private/`, `docs/tasks/`, `docs/decisions/`, `docs/knowledge/private/` or `docs/apis/private/`, wherever those directories are, naming the file. Symbolic links are followed before the test.

**The preview server** serves only regular files under `out/site/` with a known type, on loopback, to `GET` and `HEAD`; any other path, including one that climbs out of the directory, is a 404, and a path that cannot be decoded or read at all is a 400.

## The stage

[`site/stage.js`](../../site/stage.js) maps `scrollY` to a whole frame of the shot list (0.6 viewport heights of scroll per second of film), finds the shot with `shotAt`, and maps the frame inside the shot to the piece's playhead, both ends included. It draws only when the piece's own frame, the seed, the parameters or the canvas size change.

- **Scroll is never taken.** The stage reads the scroll. It writes it only when the visitor starts "Play as film" and once when a recipe URL opens; every listener is passive, and nothing calls `preventDefault`.
- **Two canvases at most.** One holds the shot on screen. The other holds the neighbour in the direction of travel: its scripts load two seconds of film before the join and its first frame is drawn one second before, so the join shows a frame already drawn.
- **Scripts load as the visitor nears a shot**: every script the shot names, in parallel, and a script already loaded for an earlier shot is not fetched again. The shot draws only once all of them have run, whether the visitor scrolled to it, jumped to it by a recipe URL or reached its still under reduced motion; the worker imports the same scripts before its first draw of the shot.
- **Three tiers.** A timed piece draws per frame on the main thread; a still draws once per change; a worker-tier shot draws in a Worker on the stage canvas, handed over with `transferControlToOffscreen`, and its poster covers it until the first frame is ready. Where that route is missing or fails, a worker-tier shot draws on the main thread once the scroll has rested for 150 ms, and never warms.
- **Scale lowering.** A main-thread shot draws at three quarters of its raster scale when at least 3 of its last 20 judged draws take over `1000 / hz - 4` ms (29.3 ms at 30 Hz), down to one device pixel per CSS pixel, and then moves to the worker tier. A cold draw, the first after the piece loads, after a new solve or on a new canvas, size or scale, is not judged, nor is a warm-up draw: a piece makes its kept copies and first marks once, and that is not what each frame costs. The draw time measured here is the time of the drawing calls, which is not the rasterization time, except with `?measure=1`, where each draw is forced to rasterize before the clock is read (see [what the checks establish](#what-the-checks-establish)).
- **Canvas size.** The piece's box fits inside the viewport, centred, at the device pixel ratio, capped at 8,294,400 pixels. A piece without `boxes` keeps its design box, fitted.
- **Boxes.** A shot whose piece declares `boxes` is shown at a box of its own, passed through `atBox` and solved again for it, so it recomposes rather than stretching. By default the box is the viewport measured at the scale the design box fits it at (`boxFor` in `stage.js`): the design box keeps its place and size on screen, and a wider or taller viewport adds room round it, so a 1280 by 800 viewport gives 1280 by 800 and a 390 by 844 one 1200 by 2597. The box is held inside the declared ranges; one they cannot hold is fitted. While such a shot is on screen, a grip at either side of its canvas can be dragged to narrow or widen the box about the window's middle, and a **Box width** slider in the bar does the same from the keyboard; the slider's full width goes back to following the window. A box chosen by a drag or the slider takes the window's height and follows it when the window is resized, never wider than the window; a box a recipe names keeps both its sides. A window made taller or shorter keeps the frame that was on screen. Because every new box is a new solve, a box that changes while the visitor drags or resizes the window is taken once they have rested for 150 ms; until then the canvas keeps its box. The canvas's label names the box. The box shot is a worker-tier shot: its solves, about 200 to 270 ms each in Node and four times that on the slow reference, and its draws run in the stage's worker, so neither the join from the contain seam nor a drag holds up a frame on the main thread. In installed Edge 157 the page's screenshots across that join, 30 frames on the desktop reference and 24 on the slow one, were the same pixel for pixel with the shot drawn on the main thread and in the worker; the main-thread draw that solved the first box had taken 156 ms and 913 ms on the two references. The worker solves the score for itself and keeps its own copies, which the kept-copies figures below do not count, and with Listen on the main thread still solves the shot once for its soundtrack.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`): nothing draws on scroll. Each shot that is not a seam shows one still, its poster or its `still` playhead drawn once as an image, as its figure nears the viewport. The preference can change without a reload. "Play as film" still plays, as an explicit request, and returns to the stills when stopped.
- **Recipes.** The address bar follows the frame on screen once the scroll has rested for 400 ms: `?shot=<name>&seed=<n>&t=<playhead>&box=<w>x<h>&p.<name>=<value>`, where `t` is the piece's quantized playhead, as the page writes it, and `box` appears only for a shot that declares boxes. Opening such a URL scrolls once to that frame; a box other than the viewport's own is kept as the chosen box. A parameter the piece refuses is dropped. "Copy link" copies it; "Re-roll seed" moves to the next seed.
- **Copy install** copies the two commands that install the plugin in Claude Code from its repository, as the README gives them, or shows them in the status line where the clipboard is refused; the last shot's text names them.
- **Take it home.** Three buttons in the bar act on the shot on screen. **Copy recipe** copies the frame's recipe as JSON (`shot`, `seed`, `t`, `params`, `box`), or shows it in the status line where the clipboard is refused. **Save picture** saves the frame as a PNG at the piece's size and **Save film** the shot's MP4, or WebM where the browser offers no H.264; each file carries its recipe, and the status line says what was saved. They load `exports.js` on the first press. One file is made at a time, and under reduced motion they ask for "Play as film" first, since a still names no frame. The 8x PNG and an SVG stay calls of `__stage.exports.file`.
- **Play as film** scrolls the site at `hz` frames a second from where it stands. The button pauses it; any key, pointer press, wheel or touch elsewhere, except on **Listen**, stops it.
- **Listen.** The site is silent until the visitor presses the **Listen** button, a toggle with `aria-pressed` whose state the status line announces ("listening", "sound off"). Pressing it makes the page's only `AudioContext`, inside that gesture; nothing renders or plays before it. Where the browser has no `AudioContext` or `OfflineAudioContext`, the button stays hidden and the site is silent. Once it is on, each shot whose piece declares `sound` has its soundtrack rendered by `renderSound` from [`core/render.js`](../../core/render.js), as the exports render it, once the scroll has rested on the shot for one second or as "Play as film" comes within two seconds of its join, never while the scroll moves, because building its graph is a long task; the last four rendered are kept, per shot, seed, parameters and box. The page plays each soundtrack as rendered, without the export's levelling.
  - *Scrolled:* the scroll plays no soundtrack. Each shot whose `sound` carries an ambience (see [sound and ambience](#sound-and-ambience)) has it rendered, as its scripts load two seconds before its join but never while the film plays, 12 seconds long plus 3.5 seconds of ring, from the same solve, and the ring is added back onto its start (`fold` in `stage.js`), so the 12 seconds loop without a seam; the last four rendered are kept, as soundtracks are. Once a shot has been the one on screen for 400 ms (`onScreen`), its ambience loops, rising over one second, and goes on at rest, scrolled back or skimmed fast, so the scroll never starts, cuts or scrubs an event. When another shot settles, its ambience crossfades in over one second as the first fades out; a shot without one, or whose ambience failed to render, fades the ambience out. A shot passed through in under 400 ms changes nothing. Because a shot can settle 400 ms after the one before, while that one's crossfade still has 600 ms to run, an ambience still fading when the next shot settles fades out over 80 ms instead: one ambience rises and one falls, and for those 80 ms at most a third is heard leaving. Switching to reduced-motion stills fades the ambience out.
  - *Played as film:* the ambience fades out over 80 ms, and comes back once the film stops. The film takes its clock from the sound the visitor hears (the audio context's output time), and starts 0.1 s after the press. While the audio context is not running, such as before it resumes or after the output device is lost, the film follows the page's clock instead, so it never stalls. Each shot's soundtrack starts at its own second of the film, the next one scheduled once rendered and within two seconds of its join; one rendered late starts where the film has got to. Pausing, stopping or turning Listen off fades the sound out.
- **Exports.** `window.__stage.exports` offers the page's exports of any shot, each file carrying its replay manifest, from `core/export.js` in `exports.js`, loaded on the first call. Each call takes `{ shot, t }`: the shot by name, the one on screen by default, and its playhead, by default the one on screen for that shot, else its `still` playhead, else 0; under reduced motion a call must name its shot. `file('png', { scale })` resolves to `{ name, blob }`, a PNG drawn `scale` times over, 1 by default; `file('svg')` to an SVG for a piece that declares vector, and refuses any other by name; `file('film', { scale, bitrate, onProgress })` to the report of the film `offer()` names, with its `name` and `blob`. `onProgress(done, total, restart)` hears every frame; `restart` is null until a pass that reported frames is dropped and the film drawn again from its first frame, and then says why, and the status line then shows the shot, the frame count and that reason. Files are named by `fileName` in `core/export.js`, as the page names them. When the film flashes more than three times in a second, the status line shows its name and the warning the page's film note gives. `offer()` resolves to `{ format, reason }` as the page's film offer: `'mp4'` where this browser encodes the film with its soundtrack, `'webm'` with the reason `'h264'` or `'soundtrack'` where it cannot, and `null` for a still. `module()` resolves to `core/export.js` itself, for `save(blob, name)`, `filmNote(report)` and `webmNote(piece, reason)`. No control on the page calls them yet.

`window.__stage` reports the mode, the frame and shot, every draw (`log`, with its shot, frame, time, tier, scale, canvas width, box (`WxH`, or null for a shot without boxes), whether it warmed a neighbour, whether it was cold and the page clock when it was drawn), Listen's state (`listen`: whether it is on, each soundtrack started with its audio time, offset and whether the film was playing, each ambience started (`rooms`), the fades, the number of soundtracks and of ambiences sounding (`playing`, `ambient`), `render(name)`, which renders a shot's soundtrack afresh, and `ambience(name)`, which renders its folded ambience afresh), the stills drawn, the scroll writes and why, scale lowering and the worker's state, and carries the stage's `force`, which the check calibrates. The check reads it.

## The product page

`index.html` is the product page, the site's landing page; the film you scroll is `film.html`, entered from the page's film card. The page follows the accepted design of entry 1419: a live hero, "How Artifex works" taught by widgets, the film card and the install commands, in a dark and a light theme.

- **Its pieces.** `site/shots.js` names them under `home`: `hero` ([`hero.cjs`](../../site/pieces/hero.cjs)), the intro's plotter writing the name, with two declared parameters, `weight` and `soak`; `teach` ([`tides.cjs`](../../site/pieces/tides.cjs)), a small piece of lines a pen lays across a sheet, which declares a seed's choices, a timeline, two parameters, boxes and vector output, so one piece serves every widget; and `ui` ([`ui.cjs`](../../site/pieces/ui.cjs)), the interface sounds, one soundtrack whose `sound.cues` names the slice each event plays. They join the module list after the shots and their heard pieces, so a module they share with a shot is defined once; `data.js` gives each `home.<name>` its id and scripts, the shared scripts it reaches first and then `home-<name>.js`. The hero shares the intro's paper and wordmark and adds only its own file.
- **The hero.** `home.js` draws it as it is written, over its 4.5 s, then holds it. The pointer across the sheet sets `weight` and down it `soak`; two sliders under it set the same values from the keyboard; New seed writes another sheet. Each change is a new solve, about 1.5 ms in Node; the paper depends on the seed alone, so `hero.cjs` keeps the first solve of each seed and puts its paper back through `held.js`. `wordmark.js` reads `weight` and `soak` from the state with defaults of 1, so the intro, which declares neither, draws as before.
- **The widgets.** Five, each the teaching piece with one idea: two sheets at one seed and a New seed button; a playhead slider and Play; a slider per declared parameter, its range from the declaration; a box width slider through `atBox`; and Save PNG, Save SVG and Save film through the same `exports.js` the film's bar uses. A widget's scripts load when it comes within 300 px of the screen.
- **The install.** The page shows each host's commands from the README: every "With <host> installed:" line and the shell block after it, cut at build time, so the page and the README cannot disagree. A README with none fails the build.
- **Sound.** Silent until Listen. - **Themes.** [`home.css`](../../site/home.css) declares the colours as tokens, the light ones on `:root` and the dark ones twice, under `prefers-color-scheme: dark` and under `data-theme="dark"`; the Theme button sets `data-theme` and keeps the choice in `localStorage`, and `theme.js`, loaded in the head, applies it before the page paints. `site/tests/themes.test.js` computes the WCAG contrast of every text pair in both themes and refuses one under 4.5:1 (3:1 for the focus ring), and checks the two dark blocks hold the same tokens.
- **Reduced motion.** Each piece is drawn once, at its last playhead, and nothing plays.

### What `tools/check-site.js` checks in Edge

`tools/check-site.js` runs in installed Edge through `withEdge` from [`tools/check-browser.js`](../../tools/check-browser.js), with its temporary profile and cleanup, against the builder's loopback server. A probe installed before the page's first script counts scroll writes, `preventDefault` on wheel, touch, scroll and key events, and listeners for them that are not passive.

**The product page.** When the site has one, the check first opens `index.html` at 1280 by 800 and at 390 by 844. At each it waits for the hero to finish writing, then requires ink on its canvas, a parameter changed by a pointer move over it, a new seed from New seed, all five widgets drawn once scrolled to, the theme toggle flipping the ground and back, no page wider than the window and no page error. Then it requires silence until Listen is pressed and a sound on the next toggle, and, under reduced motion, one draw of the hero at its last playhead. The film's phases below open `film.html`.

**The walk.** On two references, desktop (1280 by 800 at DPR 1) and slow (390 by 844 at DPR 3, a 1170-pixel canvas, 4x CPU throttling), it scrolls the whole film forward by native wheel events, one frame of scroll per event, drawing forced to rasterize (`?measure=1`): after each draw the stage reads one pixel of it through a new 1-by-1 GPU-backed canvas, which cannot answer before the frame is rasterized, and only then reads the clock. The p95 is therefore the drawing calls plus their rasterization, forced through a one-pixel GPU readback. The sink is not a 1-by-1 canvas kept for reading (`willReadFrequently`): drawing into one copies the whole stage canvas to the CPU, a cost no visitor pays.

**The sink's calibration.** Before walking, each reference calibrates the sink on a canvas the stage's size: a frame of few drawing calls and heavy rasterization (one 4,000-point stroke) is timed six times unforced, six forced by the stage's `force` and six forced by that whole-frame copy, alternated, and the walk fails unless the forced median is at least half the copied one and three times the unforced one, so a sink that stopped waiting fails instead of letting every shot pass fast (`sink` in the JSON).

The walk then requires:

- no page errors;
- three wheel steps too small for a new frame draw nothing;
- the walk ends on the last frame;
- every shot drawn;
- as many draws as distinct frames, besides one redraw of the frame on screen after each scale change, counted apart;
- at most two canvases;
- no scroll write, `preventDefault` or active listener;
- no audio context, live or offline, made without the visitor pressing Listen;
- each main-thread shot's p95 draw time, at the scale it settled on and over its draws after the cold one, within 12 ms (desktop) or 33 ms (slow) per frame, or 100 ms and 300 ms for a still. The portal has 14 ms on desktop (`FRAME_MS` in the check): its steady draws read 7.9 to 9.0 ms alone and 11.5 to 12.7 beside another browser on the same machine, and skipping its off-view arcs, with identical frames, did not lower that.

The cold draws are left out of the p95 but reported, with their times, in the JSON, so a stall once per page stays visible. Each walk also reports, as `kept` in the JSON and in the summary line, the copies `held.js` keeps on the main thread at its end and the most megapixels they held at once, from the tally it publishes as `window.__held`.

**Reduced motion, the worker fallback, recipes and Play as film.** Then:

- reduced motion must show a loaded still with alternative text for every shot that is not a seam, hide the stage and the seams, draw nothing on scroll, and return to the film when the preference changes back;
- on the shot that shows every control, each control has a name in the browser's accessibility tree, is at least 24 by 24 CSS px, is reached by Tab, shows a focus ring and is not covered where it takes the focus; every stage canvas is an image with a label; the page has a status region and says its language; and every heading, card text and control label has a contrast of 4.5 to 1 or more against its ground, a translucent card's taken over black and over white, whichever is worse;
- with scripts off, the shots' titles read in order and every shot with a poster shows it as an image with the shot's title as its text (the build writes it inside `noscript`);
- no seam flashes more than three times in any second: every frame of each is drawn at its design size and read by the reader a film export uses, which counted 2 at most (trace and contain) in installed Edge 157; drawn 160 pixels wide instead, thin marks alias from frame to frame and the bend seam read 5, so the frames are not drawn small;
- no page of the site asked for anything outside the site's own origin, through every phase of the check;
- with `transferControlToOffscreen` removed, the last worker-tier shot, where the skimming walk comes to rest, must draw on the main thread;
- a recipe URL must open its shot, frame and seed with exactly one scroll write, and rewrite the address bar;
- "Play as film" must reach within a few frames of `hz` frames after one second, stop on a key, and make no audio context.

**Listen.** Then Listen, counted by a probe installed before the page's first script that wraps `AudioContext`, `OfflineAudioContext` and every buffer a live context starts:

- with `AudioContext` removed, the button is hidden;
- every shot's soundtrack, and every ambience, rendered twice in the page, holds the same bits in every sample, naming the channel, sample and values of the first difference otherwise, and none is silent;
- no audio context exists before the press;
- Tab reaches the button, which reads **Listen** with `aria-pressed="false"`;
- Enter makes exactly one context, running, and the status line (`role="status"`) says "listening";
- at the first shot with an ambience, the ambience starts with no scroll at all; then a second of wheel steps forward at real time, a rest of 600 ms, half a second back and half a second at three times real time start, fade and cut nothing, the ambience sounding alone throughout and no soundtrack;
- two seconds more carry the scroll into the next shot; within 8 s the ambience is that shot's, or none for a shot without one, and no count sampled during that scroll or the wait finds more than two ambiences sounding, or any soundtrack (the time allowed covers rendering the next ambience on a loaded machine);
- at a recipe URL 1.5 s before the first join between two shots with ambiences, 2.5 s of scrolling at real time crossfades: both sound at once, then, within 8 s, only the second, and no soundtrack sounds; the check fails when no two shots with ambiences meet;
- at a recipe URL three seconds before the first join between two shots with sound, after a rest of 1.5 s, "Play as film" for 4.5 seconds starts both soundtracks and no ambience; the second soundtrack is built while the film plays, and the longest gap between animation frames meanwhile must be at most 200 ms; each start the stage records is a call the probe saw, and every frame drawn after its shot's soundtrack started lies within one frame (33.3 ms at 30 Hz) of the sound heard as it was drawn, the heard time taken from the probe's own reading of the context's output clock;
- pressed again, the button fades the sound, soundtracks and ambience, and the status line says "sound off".

**Exports.** Then, at a recipe URL for the first timed shot of 5 seconds or less, the stage's exports:

- `exports.js` is not fetched until the first file is asked for, and is then;
- a 1x PNG, read in Node, names the recipe the address bar names at scale 1 and has the piece's size;
- an 8x PNG has eight times the size and names scale 8;
- an SVG of a raster-only piece is refused by name;
- the film on offer is the MP4, and its bytes at scale 1, read in Node, name the piece, seed and every frame;
- with H.264 refused, as `npm run browser -- --force no-h264` refuses it, the offer is the WebM for the H.264 reason.

It does not record the WebM or replay the files; `npm run replay` of files saved from the stage does that.

**Boxes.** Last, the box path, at each reference's viewport size (1280 by 800, and 390 by 844 at DPR 3, both without throttling), for the first shot whose piece declares `boxes`:

- opened by a recipe with no box, it is drawn at the viewport's box on a canvas of that box's size, centred, with its grips, its **Box width** control and a label naming the box, and the address bar names the box;
- a grip dragged in by a quarter of the canvas with native mouse events draws it again at the narrower box and rewrites the address bar;
- that address opened afresh draws that box again.

At 1280 by 800, the window widened to 1600 by 800 gives the shot the box for that size; with a grip dragged in, the window made 1000 tall keeps the box's width, gives it the new height, and stays on the same shot and frame; and the first shot that is neither a seam nor declares boxes keeps its design box, fitted, names no box and shows no box control.

**Measured results.** The check was run in headless Edge 153 on one desktop machine while other work loaded its CPU, so its times move by several milliseconds between runs. The portal and the Kandinsky shot go over 12 ms on desktop under load, and near full load other art shots can too; otherwise every shot held its budget (see [the art shots](#the-art-shots) for each piece's figures). On the slow reference the stage lowered the portal, and in some runs bend, on their steady draws, and the draw count held. The sink calibrated at 53.7 ms forced against 58.3 ms copied whole on desktop, and 52.8 against 62.7 on the slow reference. In Edge 157 the Kandinsky shot's over-budget draws turned out to be first-time costs: a freshly opened browser paid once for each kind of knife step and each form, and a second walk in the same browser did not. The shot now draws the first step of every kind in its warm-up and every other step off screen in idle periods before the knife reaches it. With a fresh browser for each run and the two builds alternated three times, its desktop p95 went from 12.7, 12.6 and 11.9 ms to 7.4, 7.1 and 6.9 ms, and two whole checks then passed with every desktop p95 at 9 ms or under (Kandinsky 6.4 and 6.0, the portal 9.0 and 7.9) and every slow one under 24 ms. With nothing else running, the desktop walk had read 1.1 to 1.5 times slower than beside other work, and the slow walk the same; the likely cause, not proven, is a machine that clocks down between light frames. On the slow reference the fold shot is the one that flaps. While another browser's windows were busy on the same machine, the stage lowered its scale in 6 of 11 checks, once down to the worker tier: three of twenty draws had passed 33 ms, and each lower scale makes the kept copies again, which costs more draws. With those windows closed it was lowered in none of 6 checks (p95 9.1 to 23.7 ms) and in none of 4 walks of the shot alone in a fresh browser (p95 16.5 to 19.5 ms). Its heaviest draws are frames 161 to 174, where the wing's edge folds as the first tear starts, and frame 216, the first of the light's swing, 27 to 38 ms in every walk. Under load both walks of the film took about six minutes, so without `--timeout-ms` the check's deadline grows with the film: 250 ms a frame, 300,000 ms at least, which is 1,200,000 ms for the current 4,800-frame film.

**The later shots' ambiences.** Paint, and every shot of the score (unstick, cues, readout, settle, contain and the box shot), were silent while scrolled. Paint now sounds thick paint at rest, and the score's shots share one ambience, the table the framed painting lies on (`air` in `site/pieces/voices.js`), so the place stays one place: scattered noise contacts at many levels and distances and a slow dark swell now and then, as the early shots have, and no held tone. In installed Edge 157 they render the same bits twice and peak at -33.8 and -34.7 dBFS, inside the early ambiences' -31.0 to -34.2. Nobody has listened to them yet. Paint's soundtrack is voiced as its material too, as the seams before it are: every scrape of paste and every dab is a contact of noise, no pitched body, and its first dabs drag a breath of the brush; it peaks at -17.9 dBFS, where the pitched one stood at -17.6, and is held to the early seams' rules by the sound tests.

**Listen and the scroll.** An ambience's graph is built as the scroll nears its shot, which held the main thread for one long task: with Listen on, the slow reference waited 229 to 326 ms between two frames about a second before each of four joins. `ambience` in `site/pieces/sounds.js` now places its voices a few in each idle period, in the same order from the same generator, and sums them in another; the stage renders once that resolves. Installed Edge 157, the two builds alternated twice: those waits came to 104 to 132 ms, most of it the next shot's solve, which its picture needs a second later anyway, and all seven ambiences render the same bits as before.

**Listen, measured.** In Edge 154, headless, all fourteen soundtracks (the eight seams, the five pieces of shot 4 and the box shot) rendered the same bits twice, with peaks from -20.6 to -12.6 dBFS, and so did the seven ambiences, with peaks from -34.2 to -31.0 dBFS, about 13 to 18 dB under their seams' soundtracks. One earlier run had named two renders of `crack` and of `fold` that differed by 1.7e-7 and 3.1e-7 in their fade tails. In Edge 157, with other work loading the CPU, it did not come back: `crack` and `fold` rendered 25 times each, and 60 times each with the CPU throttled four times, held the same bits every time, as did eight renders of every soundtrack. The check stays exact, so a difference is still named with its channel, sample and values. Scrolled within `bloom`, its ambience sounded alone throughout, and nothing started, faded or was cut; scrolled into `ink`, it faded out; scrolled from `bend` into `portal`, the two crossfaded. Played as film from three seconds before the join from `bend` to `portal`, so that `portal`'s soundtrack was built as the film played, the 125 and 126 frames judged in two runs were drawn within 10.7 and 7.7 ms of the sound heard with them, and the picture's longest stop was 118 and 97 ms. In a separate probe, played as film from three seconds before every join into or out of the seven early seams, after a rest of 1.5 s, the longest stop was 21 to 125 ms at the joins into and out of them and 194 ms at the join from `fold` into `paint`, where the paint seam's own soundtrack, still voiced as sine partials, is built.

**Seam films and replay.** Saved with `npm run film` and checked with `npm run replay`, the `bloom` and `bend` seams' MP4 films matched their recipes, pictures and AAC soundtracks, but the films of `crack`, `portal`, `trace`, `print` and `fold` did not: in each, one block of the soundtrack decoded 10.7 to 22.9 dB from the render, under replay's 25 dB floor for AAC, where sparse contacts of noise sound. Their 24 sampled frames each measured 38.5 dB PSNR or more against their redraw, over replay's 30 dB floor, and the difference each verdict names is the soundtrack. Replay's floor was set on tonal soundtracks and on contacts made of sine partials; these seams sound their materials as noise, so their films cannot yet be proved by replay. The paint seam's film was not checked again.

**Where the portal's time goes.** The portal's slowest frames are its last two seconds, where the camera is deep in the mirror, and they are bound by the GPU, not the CPU: of a median of about 9 ms, about 2 ms is JavaScript, and the page drawn through the inversion takes about half. The fold's smooth path reads its three channels written out instead of through a function made per pixel, a quarter faster on the frames that use it, with pixels identical to a function per pixel (`site/tests/inversion.test.js`, and a frame-by-frame comparison of the portal and bend at scales 1 and 0.75 in Edge). The portal stays over the 12 ms desktop budget under load. It has not been measured on a quiet machine.

The check does not establish: how the sound sounds (no one has listened to it for this check), audio output latency on a real device (headless Edge plays to no speaker, so the output time is the browser's estimate), whether an ambience's 12-second loop is heard as a repeat or a crossfade heard by ear as smooth, behaviour on phones and tablets (the slow reference is a throttled desktop CPU on its GPU), headed windows, touch or trackpad inertia, other browsers, memory use, flashes, contrast, keyboard traversal, screen-reader output, scale lowering's effect on the art (the check records which shots were lowered, not how they look), the GPU memory kept copies take (the check counts their canvases and pixels, not what Edge allocates for them), or whether a poster matches its live frame.

### The first load

The product page's first load is 104.2 kB of brotli, 45.8 kB under the 150 kB budget: the page, `theme.js`, `home.css`, `data.js`, `core.js`, `home.js` and the hero's scripts, never `stage.js`. The film's, from `film.html`, is 113.7 kB: `core.js` is 85.2 kB of it, the library modules the stage and the first shot require, `shared-1.js` 9.1 kB, the paper and wordmark, `stage.js` 14.2 kB, Listen's playback, its ambiences and the box controls included, and the page, its data, its style sheet and `shot-intro.js` the rest (see [what the build writes](#what-the-build-writes)). A `core.js` holding the whole library would put the first load over the budget. The whole site is 914.7 kB, the posters included, of which `exports.js` is 47.6 kB.

### Kept copies

The release rule bounds what `held.js` keeps: a desktop walk of the whole film ends holding 180 copies of 14.8 megapixels and peaks at 22.5, and a slow walk, whose box shot draws a box 1200 by 2597, 185 copies of 14.3 megapixels and peaks at 21.4, both under the 24-megapixel ceiling (see [the art shots](#the-art-shots)). The box shot draws the old picture a whole number of device pixels wide, as the seam before it did: a box size that was rounded gave a scale a hair off the seam's, which keyed a second set of the painting's copies, and that slow walk then ended at 334 copies of 23.1 megapixels. The wall and the score's pieces keep one copy of the painting, of its ground and of each framed work per seed, under the first solve that made them, and the unstick seam makes its loose skins in idle time before its first form cracks (`prepare` in `loose.js`), a few in each idle period, each read back a pixel. Its warm-up draw before the join, which made all of them at once, went from about 350 ms to 36 ms on the desktop reference and from about 1000 ms to 150 ms on the slow one, measured in installed Edge 157 with the two builds alternated twice; the longest wait between two draws near the join went from about 400 to 210 ms and from 1050 to 530 ms, now the one idle period that paints the painting without its forms. Before that, each solve keeping its own held 2.5 megapixels more on both walks. Scrolling back from the portal into crack and ink, whose copies had been given up, remade them on the first draw back, the warm-up drawn off screen a second before the join: 94 ms for crack and 47 ms for ink, against steady medians of 6.4 and 5.6 ms. A warm-up is not judged; the stage does not mark the redraw cold, so a draw that remade copies without a warm-up, after a jump, would be judged. Frames drawn after the remake replay identical to a fresh draw. Releasing the copies did not change the portal's cost.
